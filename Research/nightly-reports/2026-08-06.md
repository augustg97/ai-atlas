### 5a. The decay curve, and where the day's two rules sit on it

`impact_magnitude` = `IMPACT_CLASS[minor]` (0.003) × corroboration × **0.5^repeat_k**, and
`repeat_count` counts every application of that rule in the trailing **30 days** — including
earlier applications from the same night.

| repeat_k | magnitude | share of base |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.003000 | 100% |
| 1 | 0.001500 | 50% |
| 2 | 0.000750 | 25% |
| 3 | 0.000375 | 12.5% |
| **4** | **0.000188** | **6.2%** |
| **5** | **0.000094** | **3.1%** |
| **6** | **0.000047** | **1.6%** |
| **7** | **0.000023** | **0.8%** |

Today's five sit at k = 4, 5, 6, 7, 5. Their magnitudes sum to **0.00044**. The same five events,
each first-of-its-kind, would have summed to **0.015** — the day's evidence was worth **2.9%** of
its unrepeated value. Yesterday's two k=1 rules alone carried 69% of a total six times larger.

**Novelty decay cannot tell a duplicate report from a distinct event.** It was built for the
Illinois SB 315 case, where four digests reported one enactment. Today it discounted:

- SpaceX/Tesla's **$16.8bn Terafab** chip complex — k=5, 3.1% of base
- Mirendil's **$100m Google Cloud** compute deal — k=6, 1.6% of base
- **Discovery Loop's** founding round — k=7, 0.8% of base

Three unrelated companies, three unrelated transactions, on one morning. The second was halved
against the first and the third quartered, for no reason except arrival order within the same
night. This is the corroboration mechanism's mirror image: `sources` counts independent reports of
one event and *raises* the magnitude; `repeat_k` counts independent *events* of one class and
lowers it. Nothing distinguishes the two inputs.

**Where this goes.** `ev-capital-commitment` has now fired 8 times since 2026-07-31 and enters
tomorrow at k=8 — 0.4% of base. `ev-enforcement-action` enters at k=6. Neither recovers until its
applications age out of the 30-day window, i.e. **2026-08-31 at the earliest**, and only if
nothing new fires in the meantime, which for capital commitments and enforcement actions is not a
realistic expectation. The three rules that describe the AI world's most routine daily news —
capital commitment, enforcement action, safety research — are being driven to silence *by being
right about what happens*.

That is the mechanism behind the 10× collapse in evidence-level L1 on the largest input day yet.
It is not a coverage problem and more rules will not fix it.
