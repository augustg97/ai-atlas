## 3. Finding — a rule's reach is set by its single widest term, and the widest terms are genre words

Term-level attribution on tonight's five: three matched on **one term each**, and in each case
that term is about the event — `unauthorized access`, `benchmark*`, `investment*`. The fourth and
fifth matched on `raised`+`invest` and on `published`+`evaluation*`.

`published` is the tell. Measured across the whole 2,338-event corpus, `ev-safety-research`'s
terms are:

```
published          319 / 2338  (13.6%)      <-- 62% of the rule's entire reach
evaluation*         43 / 2338  ( 1.8%)
paper*              33 / 2338  ( 1.4%)
interpretability     6 · red team 6 · model card 1 · eval suite 0 · safety case 0
```

Strip `published` and the rule's in-section reach falls **106 → 40**. `published` is not a term
about safety research; it is the verb of news. And `ev-safety-research`'s nudge is fixed at
**A3 +2 / A1 −1** — always optimistic about alignment.

Run counterfactually against the live matcher, three of tonight's residue items **would have
fired `ev-safety-research` and pushed the alignment axis toward "catch buys pause"** if the wiki
had filed them one section over:

- Nvidia **pausing** vendor-financing deals for AI cloud providers
- Epoch AI's analysis of OpenAI's revenue run rate
- Workday's quarterly subscription-revenue briefing

None of the three is about alignment. All three say "published".

Generalised over the whole library — in-section reach against the live corpus, against whether a
rule has ever fired:

```
                                    fires   in-sec reach   widest term (corpus breadth)
EVER FIRED (18)      median                          26                            36
  ev-capital-commitment              44             190    valuation*           (78)
  ev-safety-research                 26             106    published           (319)
  ev-compute-buildout                16             106    data center*        (130)
  ev-frontier-release                 4             144    releas*             (246)

NEVER FIRED (13)     median                           0                             1
  ev-big-correction                   0               0    writedown*            (0)
  ev-us-cn-agreement                  0               0    bilateral AI agreement (0)
  ev-autonomous-rd-demonstrated       0               0    autonomous AI R&D     (0)
  ev-moratorium-movement              0               0    moratorium           (20)
  ev-unemployment-spike               0               1    white-collar layoffs  (1)
  ... 8 more, all reach ≤ 5
```

**The separation is total — no overlap.** Every never-fired rule has in-section reach ≤ 5, and
**9 of the 13 have reach 0 against all 2,338 events.** So the never-fired half of the library is
not waiting for its event to arrive. It is **unmatchable against the record as the record is
written.** `ev-big-correction` has 0 reachable events out of **385** in its own section — that is
why it has never fired in 139 applications, and it is not a fact about the world being calm.

This upgrades the 08-20 observation ("the optimistic rule matches publication-genre words while
its counterweight needs the adverse word literally") from a pattern into a measurement with a
number per rule. It also explains the one-way drift directly: the rules that can fire are the
ones with a generic term, and a generic term is by construction not about direction.

**Tonight's cleanest instance of the asymmetry:** the most correction-flavoured event of the
night — **Nvidia pausing its credit-support programme for AI cloud providers, less than two
months after announcing it** — is filed under `Compute, Chips & Infrastructure`, which is
`ev-capital-commitment`'s own section. It matched nothing and went to residue, while two
straightforward investment announcements in the same and neighbouring sections each moved E1 up
and E3 down. E ended the night **+0.0849pp on "Boom holds", −0.0424pp on "Valuation correction"**.
