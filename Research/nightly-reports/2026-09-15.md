## Findings

### The date extractor severs dates at a window boundary

`frames.extract_event_date` (frames.py:236) truncates before it searches:

```python
head = item_text[:280]
m = _TEXTUAL.search(head)
```

Because the cut is applied to the **haystack** rather than to the match, a date that *begins*
inside the window but *ends* past it is destroyed, and the event is recorded as
`fell_back: true` — "the wiki stated no date" — when the wiki stated it plainly.

Tonight's International-AI-Regulation item is the clean specimen. Its `text_clean` is 422
characters; `September 15, 2026` sits at chars **269–287**. The window cuts at 280, so the
extractor is handed `...a position that became public on September 1` and finds nothing. The
event was then stamped with the digest's own `published` date — the same 2026-09-15, so the
*timeline* is unharmed, but the *metric the gate ratchets on* counts it as a failure to state a
date.

I first suspected the 300-character `plain[:300]` truncation in `emit.py:266`, since 84 % of
events sit at exactly 300 and fallbacks are over-represented there (92.0 % vs 83.2 %). **That was
a red herring and the correlation is spurious** — `plain` is the display field; the extractor
runs on `text_clean` (emit.py:258), the untruncated raw. Checking the source rather than the
emitted preview is what separated the two.

Classifying all **250** staged fallbacks against their vault source lines:

| class | n |
|---|---|
| date exists but starts at/after char 280 | 138 |
| genuinely undated | 87 |
| **severed by the window (starts < 280, ends > 280)** | **25** |

All 25 are cut mid-date-token; the earliest is 2026-04-24 and they accrue at roughly one a week.
**Two of the 25 are in tonight's 09-15 morning digest** — the Sacks/Khan item
(`September 12, 2026` at 270–288) and the CAP item (`September 15, 2026` at 269–287) — and the
gate was short by one.

Recovering that class alone: explicit 2,414 → 2,439, **90.6156 % → 91.5541 %**, which rounds to
91.6 and clears the floor with room. Note what that means: the severed class is almost exactly
the size of the gap between today's value and the 2026-07-31 baseline. It does not follow that
severing caused the whole 1.1 pp drift — the class was already ~11 items at baseline, so it
explains roughly a third of the erosion, not all of it. The remaining two-thirds is the
`beyond the window` class growing, which is a **design question** (is "the opening" the right
place to look for an event date?) rather than a bug, and is August's call.

**I did not fix it and did not file a queue task.** No queue task because the defect is Atlas
code, not wiki data — the vault is faultless here. No fix because repairing the extractor tonight
would turn the gate green *by changing the measurement the gate is built on*, on the first night
that measurement refused anything. That is precisely the move the ratchet exists to force to a
human. The fix is a two-character change in spirit — search the full text, or bound the match
rather than the haystack — but it reclassifies 25 historical events and warrants re-recording
the baseline deliberately, in a reviewed commit.

| argmax `p` | 7.327904e-06 | 7.331053e-06 (+0.043 %) |
| `agree` | 0.2954 | 0.2953 |

Evidence landed on **C, E, A, S**. The medoid moved on **R, S, P, G**. Counting changed numeric
fields in `marginals.json` against the committed copy: **R = 0, P = 0, G = 0.** Those three
distributions are bit-identical to last night, and the published medoid reports a different
position on every one of them.

The mechanism is not stochastic — `SEED = 20260731` is fixed and the ensemble is drawn with
`SEED + 7` every night. The medoid is a **selected row** of the 2,000-draw ensemble. Change any
column and a different row wins centrality, and the winning row carries its own coordinates on
every other column, including ones nothing happened to. The 53 % collapse in `p` is the tell: a
substantially less probable line won, because it was more central.

So **a medoid's per-axis reading is not a per-axis conclusion.** Last night's report recorded
"medoid unchanged, 4th consecutive night" as a stability signal; tonight's 4-axis move is mostly
not evidence about R, P or G at all. Both readings overstate what the medoid can say. The argmax,
which *is* a per-axis optimum, did not move.
