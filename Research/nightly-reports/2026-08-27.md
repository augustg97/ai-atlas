**The general finding is one level down.** Read `nightly_update.py:459` and `axes.py:3484`:

- `novelty_k(weights, rule["id"], today)` — the repeat damper counts prior firings **of that
  rule**.
- `spread=(n ** 0.5) / n` — the anti-independence damper counts tonight's incidents **of that
  rule**.
- `soft_squash(weekly_cum[ax_key], d)` — the weekly cap accumulates **per axis**.

**Every damping mechanism in the model is keyed to the RULE or the AXIS. None is keyed to the
INCIDENT.** So one real-world event that produces three differently-worded consequences arrives
as three independent observations, each freshly undamped. The per-axis cap is the only one that
could have caught this, and it missed too, because the trio split across A and C.

This is why `ev-safety-incident` took **71.5% of the night on its own**: at `k=0.26` against
`k=5.24–6.52` for everything else, its novelty factor was **0.860 against 0.159 — a 5.4×
advantage bought purely by having fired rarely**. 2026-08-22 recorded that the rules which fire
hardest fire rarely, so they arrive undamped; tonight is that, with the incident-multiplicity of
2026-08-24 stacked on top, and the two compound.
