**I am not overriding it, and `SKIP_AUDIT` stays off.** The ratchet exists to put a human here.
The decision is yours; three options, in my order of preference:

1. **Split the metric by date-kind.** Measure explicit rate among events that *could* carry an

## 3. One incident was applied twice, and the URL filter is why

Two `ev-frontier-release` entries tonight, distinct incident ids `b9f0d6d3ea1e2626` and
`cbdf81baeab56c69`, both event-dated 2026-09-03, both ×0.0007768 — and both are the GPT-6 Astra
release. `resolve_incidents` reported `collapsed: 0`.

Measured on tonight's actual fresh set:

```
IDF similarity            0.2342  (0.2904 against the 14-day corpus)
INCIDENT_SIM_URL          0.20    ← cleared
shared URL                axios.com/2026/09/03/openai-astra-gpt-6-agi-brockman
  its document frequency  4       URL_DF_MAX = 2  → discarded as a digest
shared URLs surviving     none
fallback threshold        INCIDENT_SIM 0.45 — 0.2342 falls short
same_incident             False
```

They cleared the URL-assisted threshold and would have merged. The one thing that stopped them was
the digest filter throwing the shared article away — **because four of tonight's 23 fresh events
cite it.** All four are about that one story: two release write-ups, the concealment-eval
disclosure, and the safety acknowledgement.

That is the filter inverting. `URL_DF_MAX` is meant to catch an aggregator covering many stories
(the AISN newsletter cited by five unrelated events). Document frequency cannot distinguish that
from **one story big enough to generate four events** — and the bigger the story, the more certain
its own coverage is to be reclassified as a digest. The filter fails hardest exactly where merging
matters most.

**Cost, by counterfactual rather than estimate** — re-running the resolver at `URL_DF_MAX=4`:

```
URL_DF_MAX=2 (live)  incidents 23  matched 8  ev-frontier-release: n=2, Σmag 0.0015536
URL_DF_MAX=4         incidents 22  matched 7  ev-frontier-release: n=1, Σmag 0.0013183
```

**17.8% over-application on that rule tonight** (+0.0002353). The `spread = √n/n` term absorbs most
of a double-count exactly as designed — the merged incident gains a source (2 → 3) which raises its
own magnitude — so the damage is a sixth, not a doubling. The design's claim that under-merging
"costs a little double-count" is borne out. But this is a *systematic* little, biased toward the
loudest developments, and it is not the under-merge the design anticipated: it is a filter
misfiring, not two write-ups sharing too little prose.

A second-order note worth knowing: `resolve_incidents(fresh)` computes both IDF and the URL
document-frequency over **one night's fresh set** — 23 documents tonight. The same Astra pair scores
0.2342 there and 0.2904 against the 14-day corpus. Whether two reports merge therefore depends on
how busy the night was.

**Not a defect: the Nvidia–Hugging Face repeat.** `838a8d189e13f795` applied 09-03 (×0.0004791) and
`ab87d15a2aa4d4b5` tonight (×0.0002818), same $12.93bn deal, both event-dated 09-03; the second
report reached the trunk after last night's emit. Two independent reasons this is within design:
they were never co-present in a fresh set (the resolver has no cross-night memory), and even
co-present they score 0.384 against the real 213-event corpus with no shared URL — below
`INCIDENT_SIM` 0.45. That is the honest under-merge the design documents and accepts.

*(My first attempt at that similarity returned 0.0, computed against a two-document corpus where
every shared token has zero IDF. Discarded; the 0.384 is against the real window.)*
