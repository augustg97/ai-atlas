## 3. Tonight's finding — both economy writes entered on the word `raised`, in the wrong sense

`ev-capital-commitment`'s `text_any` contains the bare verb **`raised`**. I ran the rule's own
term list against tonight's two drivers; in both cases `raised` is the **only** term that hits:

- *"Amazon **raised** prices across its Echo, Fire TV, Kindle and eero lines by up to 60 percent"*
  — a consumer-hardware price rise. Not a capital commitment in any sense. If it belongs on `E` at
  all it is evidence for `E2` "Margin squeeze", which the rule does not touch.
- *"Alibaba reported … net profit for the April–June quarter **fell 75%** year over year as it
  **raised** AI capital expenditure"* — one sentence carrying both directions at once. The capex
  half is real support for `E1`. The headline half is a 75% profit collapse, which is the `E2`/`E3`
  mechanism. The rule has one fixed vector, so it took `E1` **and pushed `E3` down**.

Both fired `E1 +2 / E3 −1`, unchanged and unchangeable: `ev-capital-commitment` has one sign
pattern across all 37 of its recorded firings, `ev-safety-research` one across 24, and
`ev-compute-buildout` one across 12. (Entries predating the `requested` field carry no vector; on
those with one, the pattern is single.)

**The sharpest pairing is inside one company on one day.** Alibaba launched a **HK$80 billion
($10.2bn) share placement on 2026-08-23 to fund AI-related development** — the single unambiguous
capital commitment in the day's record, filed under "AI Industry & Markets", the rule's own
section. It matched **nothing** and went to residue. Tested against the rule's term list it scores
zero hits: "launched … share placement … to fund" contains none of `raised`, `invest*`,
`funding round*`, `valuation*`, `commitment*`, `ipo`.

> The rule's vocabulary is about the **verb of raising**, not the **object raised**. Tonight it
> heard a price rise as a capital raise, and did not hear an equity raise at all.

This is a different failure from the 08-15 direction defect and from the 08-21 re-key defect. Those
are *a rule cannot read direction* and *the registry moved underneath the rule*. This is **a rule's
trigger is ambiguous in ordinary English**, and the ambiguity is not symmetric: `text_none` guards
`writedown*`, `wrote down`, `bubble bursts`, `impairment*` — the vocabulary of an asset writedown —
and nothing guards a **cost rise** or a **profit fall**, which is where tonight's two events sat.

Scale, measured honestly: across the 37 firings, `raised` appears in 7 driver excerpts and is the
**sole** trigger in 4 — two of which are tonight's. This is a **lower bound**: the log stores only
a 130-character driver excerpt while the live matcher reads `plain[:300]`, and 7 firings show no
term at all in the excerpt. The evidence log does **not record which term matched**, which is why
