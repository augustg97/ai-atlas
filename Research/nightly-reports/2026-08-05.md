## 5. The miscall — Texas, and why it is mechanical

`ev-compute-buildout` fired on **"Texas Governor Greg Abbott announced a moratorium on data
center approvals"** and moved **S2 (diversified build-out) +0.0016 / S3 (constrained by controls
and energy) −0.0013**. A state moratorium on data-center approvals, with ERCOT auditing the
interconnection queue, is evidence for S3. The model read it as evidence against.

This is not a judgment call the rule set got wrong. It is a mechanism defect, verified by direct
execution against the trunk's stored text:

```
rules matching the FULL text: ['ev-compute-buildout', 'ev-compute-constraint']
indices:                     [13, 14]
first-match-wins picks:      ev-compute-buildout
```

**Both rules match.** `ev-compute-constraint` matches on "interconnection queue" — one of its own
`text_any` terms, present in the visible 300 characters. It loses because it sits at index 14 and
`ev-compute-buildout` at index 13, and `nightly_update.py:216` **`break`s on the first match**. An
event applies at most one rule, and "first" means *list position*, not *specificity*. There is no
tie-break, no specificity score, no both-fire-and-cancel path.

Two contributing weaknesses, both in the Atlas repo:
- `ev-compute-buildout.text_none` is `["shortage*", "export control*", "denied", "halted"]` — it
  has no guard for a restriction framing. "moratorium", "pause", "audit", "ban" all pass.
- `ev-compute-constraint.text_any` spells the term **`"moratorium on datacenter*"`**, one word.
  The record says "data center", two words. Verified: the one-word spelling matches, the two-word
  spelling does not. That trap is already documented in HANDOFF (stems and whole words) and it
  bit again. It is not the cause here — "interconnection queue" carried the match anyway — but it
  would be the cause on any item without a second constraint term.

**Neither is a wiki data defect. No queue task filed.** The wiki recorded the event accurately and
in full; the Atlas mis-classified it.
