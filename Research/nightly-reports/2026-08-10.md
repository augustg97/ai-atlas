| magnitude | 0.0003768 | **0.0004748** |
| applied | `E.E1 +0.0005023` / `E.E3 −0.0002512` | `E.E1 +0.0006331` / `E.E3 −0.0003166` |

The two records are not contradictory — $2B initial for roughly a 20% stake plus $1B contingent is
the same deal as "up to $3B", and both wiki entries are accurate. That is what makes this a
mechanism finding rather than a data defect.

The r2 incident resolver collapses duplicate **reports within one night's batch**
(`resolve_incidents(fresh)`). Duplicates that arrive on *different* nights are separate incidents,
and the only thing standing between them and full double-counting is the novelty floor. The floor
worked — k rose 5.146 → 6.682 — **and the second application was still 26% larger than the first**,
because `spread` moved 0.5774 → 1.0000. On 08-08 the deal competed with two other capital
commitments and was damped by 1/√3; tonight it arrived alone and was not damped at all.

The `spread` mechanism exists so that the third funding round of an evening is not discounted by the
order the trunk happened to list it in. Its side effect, visible here for the first time, is that
**a repeat of a story lands harder on a quiet night than the original did on a busy one** — the
opposite of what the novelty floor is for. Zero reports have been collapsed on any of the five
nights the resolver has run; cross-night duplication is doing the work the collapser was built to
catch, and `spread` is partly cancelling the floor that is supposed to catch it.
