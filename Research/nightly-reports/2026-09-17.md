## Findings

### The evidence classifier reads the display preview

`emit.py:266` emits each event's `"text": plain[:300]`. That is a display truncation, and
`axes.match_event` (axes.py:2955) reads it for `text_any`, `text_all` and `text_none`.
`axes.rule_specificity` (axes.py:2988) reads the same field to rank competing rules, and
`nightly_update.salient` (nightly_update.py:155) builds incident-clustering tokens from it.
**2,279 of 2,711 items (84.1%) are longer than 300 characters.** Past character 300, no inclusion
term, no exclusion term and no specificity point exists.

This is the 09-15 extractor defect's second instance: `frames.py:244` bounds the date search at
280, and `emit.py:266` bounds the classifier at 300. Both cut the haystack.

**How it was measured.** I reparsed the whole dev-log with emit's own parser, keeping the full
cleaned text beside the 300-char field, then ran `axes.classify` on both. The reconstruction is
faithful: on the preview it reproduces the logged rule for **233 of 234** logged applications. For the
one exception, the logged rule does not win on today's rules on either text. I did not
investigate why. Scripts and output were kept in
the session scratchpad. Nothing in the repo or the vault was modified.

**The two misapplications now in the weights**, both of the class `text_none` was written for in
r2, the 2026-08-03 Texas moratorium read as buildout:

| logged | item | length | "moratorium" at | preview | full text |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 08-04 | States that had welcomed data centres moved to **repeal sales-tax breaks**, and Hochul's EO created a **statewide moratorium of up to one year** | 1,273 | char 426 | buildout, **0.0090 abs** | `ev-compute-constraint` (the opposite direction) |
| 09-16 | Hochul proposes ~$1m/MW community investment; NY "became the first US state to impose a moratorium" | 519 | char 474 | buildout, 0.0007 abs | `ev-capital-commitment` |

17 items won a rule on the preview that a `text_none` term past character 300 excludes: 7
`ev-compute-buildout` (moratorium ×4, shortage ×2, delayed ×1), 5 `ev-safety-research` (jailbreak,
exfiltration, scheming) and 5 `ev-frontier-release` (plans to, expected to, reportedly). Of the
**234 logged applications, 20 disagree on full text**, carrying about 0.065 of the log's 0.522
total absolute applied weight (≈12%).

**Full text is not the fix by itself, and the 340 are not 340 errors.** Every rule vocabulary was
tuned "on the trunk", meaning on this preview. On full text, **four logged `ev-safety-incident`
applications flip to `ev-safety-research`** with the incident rule only contested: Meta's model
hacking another company (08-06), OpenAI's Hugging Face breach report (08-27), the researchers on
that access (08-28), and Greenblatt's dispute of the Astra account (09-06). The first three are
incident reports on any reading. They flip because `rule_specificity` adds a point per matched term, so a longer text
favours whichever rule has the most generic vocabulary. Among the residue → applied cases, Microsoft
*tightening* access to Nvidia GPUs (04-24) reads as buildout. Widening the haystack moves the
precision problem somewhere else.

**Not fixed.** Changing the classifier's input rewrites what every rule means, and the weights are
cumulative, so a fix also forces a decision on whether to replay history or go forward only. That
needs a reviewed round. No queue task, because this is Atlas code and the vault text is faultless.
