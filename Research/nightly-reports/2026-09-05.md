### The mechanism, measured

`worldlines.medoid` maximises the sum over axes of how many other sampled lines share each
position — a per-axis modal objective restricted to lines that were actually drawn. So it is
decided by which position wins each axis's *sampled count*, and that is a coin flip wherever two
positions are separated by less than the sampling error at n = 2,000:

```
ax  top1 top2   p1      p2      gap       SE(n=2000)
P   P2   P3     0.2326  0.2292  +0.0034   0.0188   COIN FLIP  (5.5× inside the error)
A   A2   A7     0.2377  0.2265  +0.0111   0.0189   COIN FLIP
S   S3   S1     0.2508  0.2365  +0.0143   0.0192   COIN FLIP
L   L4   L2     0.2257  0.2105  +0.0152   0.0185   COIN FLIP
R   R2   R4     0.2475  0.2101  +0.0374   0.0188   2.0 SE
G   G2   G1     0.2584  0.1939  +0.0645   0.0186   3.5 SE
T   T3   T2     0.3312* 0.2851  +0.0461   0.0206   2.2 SE   (*marginal mode is T2)
K   K1   K2     0.5148  0.2542  +0.2606   0.0209   stable
C   C1   C2     0.3301  0.1942  +0.1359   0.0194   stable
D   D2   D3     0.3580  0.2499  +0.1081   0.0204   stable
E   E3   E1     0.3207  0.2325  +0.0881   0.0199   stable
```

Four axes are inside sampling error and a fifth (R) is at two. `agree` sits at 0.296–0.304 in
every draw: no sampled line is meaningfully closer to the ensemble than several hundred others,
so the selection is a near-flat argmax over a noisy objective. **A fixed seed does not pin
per-axis draws** — once any axis's probabilities change, the RNG stream re-aligns and every axis
resamples, which is how R and P moved on zero evidence.

**This is the 2026-09-04 observation promoted from suspicion to measurement.** The published
medoid is sampling noise on at least five of eleven axes; the argmax is stable. The delta panel
attributes the night's motion to evidence correctly (L1 0.0093 across six axes) — it is the
*drawn line* that is unreadable coordinate by coordinate. Nothing was changed in response; this
is a design call for you.

**I audited the eight witness rows tonight and the extractor is sound — but the audit was wrong
first, which is the more useful half.** Reading the stored rows, three looked plainly wrong:
`Idaho|y2026` carried an NCSL survey sentence with no Idaho in it, `Montana|y2026` carried a report
about *pending* preemption bills naming New Hampshire, and `Nebraska|#525` carried a sentence about
the **New York** State Senate. Running the extractor's own functions over those stored strings
returned no enactment verb and, for two of them, no state name at all.

The stored `text` field is truncated to **180 characters** for display. Against the full event text
all eight rows are correct: Idaho binds to "…; Idaho enacted a law requiring chatbots to refer
suicidal users to crisis centers" at character 211, Montana to "…; Montana enacted a Right to
Compute Act" at 287, Nebraska to its own enactment clause at 255. The proximity rule documented in
`witness_statelaw.py` — bind the enacting state to the nearest state name to the enactment verb —
did exactly its job, correctly declining New Hampshire, Ohio, South Carolina and Virginia in the
Montana sentence, which are the states *considering* bills. **Rule 8 earned its place: the audit
was checked first and the audit was what was broken.**

**What the corrected audit does find is a second, undisclosed undercount mechanism.** `emit.py:266`
stores `"text": plain[:300]` — every event's prose is capped at 300 characters — and three of the
eight rows have their enactment verb deep inside that window:

```
key                verb at   margin to the 300-char cap
Montana|y2026      287       6 characters
Nebraska|#525      255       38
Idaho|y2026        211       82
Maryland|#895      24        266
Colorado|#189      30        261
Illinois|#315      26        265
Illinois|#5511     26        257
New Jersey|y2026   11        219   (text 237, not truncated)
```

Montana cleared the cliff by **six characters**. All three of the deep rows are digest paragraphs
that lead with a survey or a roundup and report the enactment in a trailing clause — exactly the
shape most likely to fall past 300. So the witness is a lower bound for **two** reasons and only
one is disclosed: the documented one (the wiki tracks policy-notable developments, not all fifty
states' output), and an undocumented one (an enactment reported after character 300 of its digest
paragraph is invisible to the matcher). The second cannot be sized from staged data — the
truncation happens at emit, so the full `plain` text is gone by the time anything downstream sees
it. Measuring it needs one pass over the vault's untruncated prose, and that is the next research
item alongside the NCSL ingest.
