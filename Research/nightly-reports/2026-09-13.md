## FINDING — the forecast layer is running two different distributions

This is the night's real result and it bears directly on the pending medoid → argmax swap.

**`worldlines.joint_probability()` silently drops 92 of the registry's 213 declared conditionals
— 43 %.** It applies tilts in a single forward pass over the axis order, consulting only parents
already assigned (`if parent_pos in chosen.values()`). Every edge whose parent sits later in the
order `T K A C R D S P E L G` is lost. L is last, so all nine of its child edges are dropped;
T is first, so every edge into T from anywhere is dropped.

`axes.sample_one()` was fixed for exactly this failure on 2026-08-13 — it now Gibbs-samples,
re-drawing each unpinned axis against **all** the others for four sweeps, and its docstring names
the three edges that had been dead (S given E3, S given E4, P given E4). `joint_probability`'s
docstring still says *"same tilt arithmetic as the sampler"*. That has been false since 2026-08-13.

The consequence is a frame split, and it is the expensive class CLAUDE.md warns about:

- the **ensemble, bands, exemplars and the published medoid** are drawn from the full 213-edge
  specification;
- the **argmax line and every published `p`** are computed under a 121-edge one.

They are not two summaries of one distribution. They are summaries of two.

**Measured, not inferred.** Scoring four candidate lines under the published forward-pass rule and
under an order-independent full-conditional rule (the product of full conditionals — what the
Gibbs sampler actually uses; a pseudo-likelihood, not a normalised joint, and labelled as such):

| line | forward-pass `p` | full-conditional | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| published medoid | 9.0779e-07 | 1.2357e-06 | 1.361 |
| joint argmax (as published) | 7.3188e-06 | 9.2162e-06 | 1.259 |
| prior per-axis argmax | 1.7527e-06 | 1.2092e-06 | **0.690** |
| ensemble per-axis argmax | 6.2507e-07 | 7.7280e-07 | 1.236 |

The correction is **non-uniform — 0.690× to 1.361×**. A uniform bias cannot reorder lines; a
non-uniform one can, and here it does: among the 2,000 drawn lines, the top line under the
corrected score differs from the top line under the published score **on 4 of 11 axes (T, D, S, P)**.

A concrete mechanism on a live disagreement: the published line reads S3, and **`S3 → T` is one of
the dropped edges** (T1 0.67, T2 0.85, T3 1.11, T4 1.26). It tilts T toward T3 — the medoid's
reading — and the scorer that picks the argmax cannot see it. The argmax reads T2 in part because
that edge is invisible to it.

**What this does and does not settle for the swap.** Under the corrected score the argmax still
beats the medoid (9.2162e-06 vs 1.2357e-06, 7.46× against the published 8.06×), so the *direction*
of the swap survives. What does not survive is the **identity of the line it would publish** — and
that is precisely the thing the swap ships. Per memory, the swap already stood on "removes noise,
creates no signal"; tonight adds that the object it would publish is selected by a scorer running
on 57 % of the model.

**Limit, stated plainly:** the corrected argmax over the full 120,960,000-cell space has *not*
been computed. Branch-and-bound's admissible bound (`_axis_bounds`) is built on the forward-pass
factorisation, so it cannot be reused unchanged. What is established is the drop, its size, its
non-uniformity, and that it reorders the drawn ensemble's top line.

### Secondary — the medoid is a sample maximum over a space it barely touches

- **All 2,000 draws are distinct.** Not one line repeats. The published medoid appears exactly
  once; the argmax line appears **zero** times; the prior per-axis argmax appears **zero** times.
- The medoid's own objective is mean per-axis agreement with the ensemble. The published medoid
  scores **0.2952**. The unconstrained optimum of that same objective — the ensemble per-axis
  argmax, `T2 K1 A7 C1 R2 D2 S1 P3 E3 L4 G2` — scores **0.3142**, a **6.0 % shortfall**. The
  medoid is the best line among the 2,000 that happened to be drawn, not the best line under its
  own criterion.

That is the mechanism behind the reseeding spread recorded as "structural" on 09-12: with zero
repeats in 2,000 draws from a 120,960,000-cell space, which line is medoid is decided by which
near-optimal lines the sampler happened to reach. Reseeding replaces the entire candidate pool.

### Third — the published bars are priors; the drawn line is not

`marginals.json.today` is `axes.marginals(reg)`: the normalised per-axis priors, no coupling at
all. The ensemble's empirical marginals differ from them by up to **8.43 pp**:

| | prior (published bar) | sampled (coupled) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | 0.5148 | 0.4305 | −8.43 pp |
| S1 | 0.2491 | 0.3255 | +7.64 pp |
| D2 | 0.3604 | 0.4240 | +6.36 pp |
| R2 | 0.2475 | 0.2965 | +4.90 pp |

At n=2,000 the standard error near p=0.5 is 1.12 pp, so these are 4–7 σ. They are the coupling's
effect, not sampling noise — which is the point: the bars do not show it.

`web/index.html:716` — `activeMarginals()` returns `condCache.marginals` when a condition is
active and `D.marginals.today` otherwise, and the position rows at line 294 render whichever it
gets. So the bars are **coupled under conditioning and uncoupled without it**, and nothing on
screen distinguishes the two. A reader comparing the drawn line against the unconditioned bars is
comparing it against a distribution the line was not drawn from — which is also why the published
line's A reading (A4, 16.06 % on the bar, third on its own axis behind A2 at 23.82 % and A7 at
22.57 %) looks stranger on screen than it is.

None of this is a wiki data defect, so nothing was filed to `Wiki/_meta/queue/`. It is a model
defect and it is recorded here.
