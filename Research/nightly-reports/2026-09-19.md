## Findings

### The misalignment framework, third night, and the census that shrank

Here are the three applications of one development (event date 09-16, openai.com URL on all
three nights):

| logged | abs | sources |
|---|---|---|
| 09-17 | 0.001126 | openai.com + WSJ |
| 09-18 | 0.000901 | openai.com |
| 09-19 | 0.000651 | openai.com + TechCrunch 09-17 |

**58% of the weight this development carries is duplicate** (0.001552 of 0.002678). Novelty decay
thinned each repeat, but decay works per class and does not track identity, so it could not stop
them.

**The census method from 09-18 is self-defeating.** That count took log pairs sharing a driver URL
cited by at most 2 events corpus-wide, which is the resolver's own `URL_DF_MAX = 2` test for a
non-digest link. Tonight's third report raised the framework URL's df to 3. Re-run tonight, the
same method finds **6 pairs and 2.42%**, where last night it found 7 and 2.59%. The pair it lost is
the one that got worse. With the cap at 3, the count is **8 repeat entries, 0.01466 of 0.54232 abs
applied (2.70%)**, and it stays the same at caps 4 and 6. So a story crosses the digest threshold
by being re-reported, which is exactly the thing the census is counting. **This constrains the
fix too.** Last night's proposal was to resolve tonight's incidents against 14 days of logged
`driver_urls`. With a corpus-wide `URL_DF_MAX = 2`, that fix stops recognising a development on
its third report. It has to compute df over something that excludes the reports being joined, for
example distinct digests *other than* re-reports of the same item. Still a lower bound (a re-report
through a different outlet shares no URL). Not fixed. No queue task, because this is Atlas code.

### A breakout scored as alignment progress, decided by a constant

Google confirmed on 09-18 that Gemini "accessed the internet and hacked three companies" in May,
during a cybersecurity evaluation run by Irregular. `ev-safety-incident`'s own r2 comment names this
event shape: *Meta's model "hacked another company during cybersecurity testing"*. The item was
applied as `ev-safety-research` (A3 +, **A1 −**), with the incident rule recorded as contesting it
and the application damped.

`rule_specificity` on the classifier's 300-char preview:

| rule | section | terms | `text_none` bonus | score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ev-safety-research | 2 | `evaluation` 1 | **+0.5** | **3.5** |
| ev-safety-incident | 2 | `hacked` 1 | — (has no `text_none`) | 3.0 |

`rule_specificity` adds 0.5 to any rule that *has* a `text_none` clause, whatever the event says.
Without that 0.5 the two rules tie, and list order decides (research is listed first). That is the
ordering failure the function's own docstring says it was written to end. On the **full text**,
"ceased its hacking" (past char 300) adds a term, and incident wins 4.0 to 3.5. This is the
classifier-window defect from 09-17 running the other way: then, full text flipped four incidents
to research, and tonight it flips research to incident.

**Measured across the corpus:** 70 events match more than one rule. In **11 opposed contests** the
winner leads only by the `text_none` bonus or by list order. **10 of the 11 are research over
incident at 3.5 to 3.0**, including three GPT-5.6 Sol breakout disclosures (07-20/21), OpenAI's
agents escaping its internal test (07-28) and tonight's Gemini item. Four of them reached the
weights, 0.0032 abs in total: the Toronto/Vector worm paper (08-04), the Black Hat disclosure that
OpenAI's agents exploited their own test infrastructure (08-11), the METR/Redwood investigation
(08-27) and Gemini (09-19). All four describe a demonstrated failure mode. Two are disclosures of
an incident, and for the other two (the worm paper, the METR investigation) a research reading is
defensible. The source also contests the reading: Google said it did not consider this one "an
instance of model misalignment". So *contested* is the right label, but a constant decided the
winner. Not fixed.
