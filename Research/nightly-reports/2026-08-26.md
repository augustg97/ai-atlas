Both texts contain `enterprise`, which is in the rule's `text_any`. The **only** difference is the
section, and `ev-enterprise-adoption` gates on
`section_any: ['AI Adoption by Industry', 'Labor, Society & Democratic Institutions']`.

Verified by counterfactual, not inferred — refiling the residue event under the gated section and
re-running the live matcher:

```
Gemini pay-as-you-go AS FILED (AI Industry & Markets)      -> NO MATCH
Gemini pay-as-you-go IF FILED under AI Adoption by Industry -> ['ev-enterprise-adoption']
```

**The generalisation.** 08-25 established that 8 of 11 sections have exactly one eligible rule, so
the news hook fixes the vector before a word is read. Tonight isolates the variable: with subject,
vocabulary, company, product and week all held constant, **a wiki editor's filing choice was the
whole difference between moving three positions and being unheard.** The section is metadata about
where a human put a story; the rule library treats it as evidence about what the story is.

The same probe run on the Bill Gates memo (08-26, `Labor, Society & Democratic Institutions`,
arguing AI is advancing faster than governments and society are prepared for) shows the knife cuts
both ways:

```
Gates memo AS FILED                              -> NO MATCH
Gates memo IF FILED under AI Safety, Alignment   -> ['ev-safety-research']
```

`ev-safety-research` matches on the bare word `published` and its fixed vector is A1 −1, A3 +2 —
"alignment is tractable with effort". Filed one section over, a memo about society being
**unprepared** would have pushed the model toward tractability. The section gate blocked a
backwards move here by accident, which is the same mechanism that silenced the Gemini item. A gate
that is right for the wrong reason is not a guard.
