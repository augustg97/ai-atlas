### 1. The published mainline is a sampling statistic whose seed-to-seed spread is larger than a night of evidence — and twelve seeds give twelve different lines

The line on the sheet is **not** the argmax. `forecast_emit.py:230-232` draws a 2,000-line Gibbs
ensemble and publishes `worldlines.medoid(ens)`; the exact argmax is carried beside it. Tonight
the medoid moved on four axes and the argmax did not move at all.

I held the registry completely fixed — tonight's weights, tonight's widening — and varied only
the ensemble seed:

```
seed  20260738  T3·K1·A4·C1·R4·D2·S3·P3·E3·L4·G2   agree=0.2950   <- PUBLISHED
seed  20261738  T2·K1·A7·C1·R2·D2·S1·P4·E3·L2·G4   agree=0.2955
seed  20262738  T2·K1·A2·C1·R2·D2·S1·P4·E3·L4·G2   agree=0.3078
seed  20263738  T3·K1·A7·C1·R4·D2·S1·P3·E3·L2·G4   agree=0.2977
…12 seeds → 12 DISTINCT medoids, not one repeat
```

Eight of eleven axes are unstable to reseeding alone (T 6:6, A 7:4:1, R 8:4, S 8:4, P 6:4:2,
L 7:5, G 8:3:1, D 11:1). Only K, C and E held. The published line is itself an outlier among its
own siblings: **A4 appeared in 1 of the 12 draws** — the published seed's.

The decisive test is the 2×2. Same seed, one night of evidence apart:

| | published seed | seed+1000 |
|---|---|---|
| **09-10 weights** | `T3·K1·A4·C1·R2·D2·S1·P2·E3·L4·G1` | `T2·K1·A7·C1·R2·D2·S1·P4·E3·L2·G4` |
| **09-11 weights** | `T3·K1·A4·C1·R4·D2·S3·P3·E3·L4·G2` | `T2·K1·A7·C1·R2·D2·S1·P4·E3·L2·G4` |

At seed+1000 the two nights give the **identical** line, character for character. Across ten
seeds, asking "how many axes did tonight's evidence move the published mainline by":

```
published seed: 4 axes.   The other nine seeds: 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0.
```

**Nine of ten seeds would have reported "mainline unchanged."** The variable deciding whether
tonight's forecast "moved its mind about regulation, safety posture, policy and governance" is
the seed's interaction with the sampler, not the six applications.

`agree` held at 0.295–0.308 across every seed, so the flatness is a property of the distribution
and not of one unlucky draw: the medoid's own positions are shared by under 30% of lines, and on
a landscape that flat a tiny perturbation moves a discrete argmin a long way.

**This is the fifteenth instance of the window-label defect class, and a new variant of it.** The
previous fourteen were a number compared to its own past with the population silently moving
underneath. This one is a number compared to its own past where the *population is fixed and the
statistic is noisy* — and the noise was never measured, so "the mainline is unchanged"
(2026-09-10) and "the mainline moved on four axes" (tonight) were never distinguishable claims.
The report has been reading tea leaves on this line for as long as it has been reporting it.

**The stable object was in the file the whole time.** `mainline.json.argmax` is deterministic,
has no sampling noise, and was byte-identical on both nights. Its probability fell 0.83%, which
is the actual, readable signal of what a night of evidence did.

### 2. The trunk holds the California signing twice, and the forecast paid for it twice

`ev-state-law-enacted` fired at **notable** tier on the California signing for the **second
consecutive night**, on the same event date (2026-09-09), under a different incident id:

```

run 2026-09-11  incident 9ba8e66d7e30a03e  mag 0.004687  k=0.963  src=1
```

These are two trunk events, both dated 2026-09-09, both in section "U.S. State AI Legislation",
both sharing the gov.ca.gov press-release URL, describing one signing:

> #1 `Newsom signed Senate Bill 813, authored by Sen. Jerry McNerney (D-Pleasanton), and…`
> #2 `California Gov. Gavin Newsom signed Senate Bill 813 and Assembly Bill 1405 into law on…`

**Why neither guard caught it.** `nightly_update.fingerprint()` is
`sha1(date | section | text[:200])` — the prose is *part of the identity*, so a re-worded report
of the same development is a new event to the `seen` ledger. Its docstring promises
collision-freedom ("verified collision-free across all 1,894 events"), which is the guarantee
that two events never share one id; the opposite failure — one event acquiring two ids — was
never in scope. And `same_incident()` *would* have merged these on their shared URL and anchors,
but `resolve_incidents` runs **within a night's batch**: the twins arrived on different nights,
so the merge machinery that exists for exactly this never saw them together. Both applications
carry `reports=1`, so the model also scored one signing as two uncorroborated incidents rather
than one corroborated one — mis-scaling corroboration in both directions.

**The novelty damper is the only thing that fired**, and it is keyed on the rule rather than the
text, so it did see a repeat: `k` rose 0.064 → 0.963 and cut the second application to 49% of
the first. One signing still moved C by **0.014320 combined**, of which 33% was the replay.

**The same duplicate hit the state-law witness and bounced off.** `law_keys` returns
content-addressed `state|bill` keys, so both copies collapse to one `California|#813`. Same
defect in the data, two subsystems, opposite outcomes — the difference is that one identifies
events by their prose and the other by what the prose is *about*.
