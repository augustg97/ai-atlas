## 3. Finding — the digest heuristic disqualifies primary sources, and it split the night's largest event in two

Tonight's biggest application, `ev-frontier-release` on the Fable 5.1 / Mythos 5.1 release, has a
twin. **The same release is also sitting in tonight's residue as an unexplained incident.**

```

residue  fp 72936592dab03660  urls: anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1               [Frontier Models & Capabilities]
```

Both are 2026-09-01, both in section `Frontier Models & Capabilities`, both describing one
announcement. `resolve_incidents` left them as two incidents. Reproduced exactly against last
night's `weights.json` from git: **23 fresh, 20 incidents, 3 collapsed** — the run's own numbers —
with the two reports in incidents 0 and 10.

The 08-06 design note says the resolver **under-merges by design**, so the first question is whether
this is that. It is not. Measured on the pair, in the resolver's own frame:

```
similarity            0.4029     thresholds: URL-assisted 0.20 · prose-only 0.45 · follow-up 0.36
shared anchors        3          guard requires 2                      -> PASSES
shared url            https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1
  udf(that url)       4          URL_DF_MAX = 2                        -> DISQUALIFIED
qualifying urls       none
same_incident()       False
```

The pair clears the anchor guard and clears the URL-assisted similarity threshold by a factor of
two. It fails on one thing: **Anthropic's own announcement page for the model is classified as a
digest**, because four events cite it.

The heuristic was built for newsletters, and the comment names them — `newsletter.safe.ai`'s AISN-77
cited by five events, `transformernews.ai` by four, because *"a newsletter covers many stories."*
That reasoning does not transfer. A canonical announcement page is cited by many events for the
opposite reason: the wiki splits **one** announcement into several developments — here the model
release, the data-retention replacement, and two more — and each cites the one authoritative source.
Document frequency cannot tell breadth of coverage from depth of coverage, and the strongest
identity evidence in the corpus is the evidence most likely to be thrown away.

**The class is 53 pairs, corpus-wide.** Pairs that share a URL, pass the anchor guard, clear the
0.20 URL-assisted threshold, and are blocked *only* because that URL's `udf > 2`:

```
53 pairs · by host:  anthropic.com 15 · openai.com 7 · reuters.com 4 · theinformation.com 4
                     cnbc.com 3 · wsj.com 2 · insideaipolicy.com 2 · darioamodei.com 2
                     ai.meta.com 2 · variety.com 2 · (rest 1 each)
```

The top ten by similarity are, read in full, ten pairs of write-ups of one development each: the
Snap layoffs (0.437), the Anthropic Institute essay (0.437), Thinking Machines' Inkling release
(0.413), the WSJ shooting story (0.413), California SB 813 (0.408), Zuckerberg's town hall (0.403),
Fable 5.1 (0.403), the OpenAI/Hugging Face disclosure (0.403), Cursor's "Sand" (0.394), Opus 4.8
(0.391). **16 of the 99 currently-disqualified URLs are lab or vendor first-party announcement
pages** — `anthropic.com` ×9, `openai.com` ×4, `ai.meta.com`, `x.ai`, `huggingface.co` — every one a
model release or an incident disclosure.

**What it cost tonight, stated exactly.** Less than it looks, and the honest number matters. Merged,
the incident's distinct domains would still be two (`anthropic.com`, `axios.com`), so
`corroboration` stays ×1.25 and the magnitude would not have changed. Across all 53 pairs, merging
would raise the corroboration multiplier in **6 (11%)**. The real costs are the other two:

- **One spurious residue entry**, in `Frontier Models & Capabilities` — a section with 4 entries in
  the current 7-day window, which the Monday schema review reads as a cluster of *unexplained*
  events. One of those four is a report of the single best-explained event of the week.
- **31 of the 53 pairs have a single-domain half.** `ev-frontier-release` is the **only** rule in
  the registry carrying a `min_sources` gate (2), and the orphaned twin fails it: verified by
  matching the rule against that event as-is (`False`) and against a copy with one extra source URL
  (`True`). So for this rule specifically, a bad split does not merely lose corroboration — it
  strands a report below the rule's own floor.

**The fix, and a measurement that it separates.** Per rule 2, the level that fixes the class is not
the constant. Document frequency is the wrong statistic; the right one is whether the events citing
a URL are about the same thing. Mean pairwise IDF similarity of each URL's citing set, over the 99
disqualified URLs:

```
the 11 newsletters / click-trackers named in the code   coherence 0.005 – 0.030
the 18 lab & vendor announcement pages                  coherence 0.073 – 0.363
```

Nothing between 0.030 and 0.073. A coherence floor around 0.05 admits every first-party announcement
page and excludes every newsletter the comment was written about, including
`click.mail.fortune.com` (udf 13, coherence 0.012) and `theinformation.com/ls/click` (udf 14, 0.016)
— the two worst chainers in the corpus. **This is a separation on 29 labelled URLs, not a proof.**
Two `substack.com/redirect` URLs land at 0.133 and 0.187, inside the first-party band; they are
opaque redirects and should be excluded on URL shape regardless of coherence. And the 08-06 chaining
disaster stays contained either way, because `same_incident` still requires 2 shared anchors and
similarity ≥ 0.20 before a qualifying URL means anything.

```
T1  2027–2028   T2  2029–2031 (mainline)   T3  2032–2036   T4  2037–2050   T5  Method asymptote
```

So **every frontier release, whatever was released, pushes probability toward 2032–2036 and away
from 2037–2050** — and by normalisation away from everything else. Tonight that meant Anthropic
shipping a new frontier model **lowered** the modelled probability of the two fastest tempo
positions: `T2 −0.0468pp` (the mainline) and `T1 −0.0140pp`. T1 is the sole support position for the
registered claim `cl-sc-2027` (§6).

identity, not by the release. This is the third instance of the same family: `ev-safety-incident`
choosing A1 by keyword (08-31 §3), the six domestic rules choosing C by rule id (§5), and now a
tempo rule choosing a five-year bracket by constant.
